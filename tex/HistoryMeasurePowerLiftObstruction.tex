\begin{abstract}
A finite branching system is a finite set of local states equipped with an admissibility
relation and a row-stochastic transition kernel. From an initial law, such a system
induces a canonical positive valuation on admissible histories. We prove that this
valuation is the unique member of its own power-lift family, $\mu^\alpha$ for
$\alpha>0$, that is \emph{Kolmogorov-consistent}: summing the lifted weight of every
admissible one-step extension of a history recovers the lifted weight of the history
itself, so that $\{\mu_n^\alpha\}_n$ forms a projective family of measures. The proof is
an elementary comparison of $x^\alpha$ against $x$ on $[0,1]$, strict at every state with
at least two admissible successors. Consequently, no nonlinear power lift of a positive
branching measure --- in particular no square-root (``amplitude'') lift --- defines a
projective family in the sense of the Kolmogorov extension theorem, and this failure is
established before any question of phase, complex structure, or the Born rule is posed.
We instantiate the obstruction on the non-backtracking Heisenberg-word automaton of the
Cosmochrony trajectory-branching programme~\cite{Beau2026tb}, under an explicitly named,
non-derived maximal-entropy/Parry postulate~\cite{Parry1964} we call \textup{H2},
computing the obstruction exactly in $\Qroot$. We further show, with explicit witnesses
on words of length up to~$4$, that the endpoint quotient and the shadow quotient of this
automaton are mutually incomparable; verify two exact covariances of the resulting phase
diagnostic; and report a bounded exact search, on words of length up to~$8$, finding no
destructive cancellation among histories sharing an endpoint. No result here concerns the
Born rule, a complex carrier, or a coherent-sum construction; each remains exactly as
open as it was in the companion note this paper succeeds~\cite{Beau2026steft}.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}
Kolmogorov consistency; power lift; Markov measure; Parry measure; maximal entropy;
refinement consistency; Heisenberg group; Weil representation; non-backtracking words;
amplitude obstruction.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

A recurring move, whenever a finite process produces positive weights on branching
histories, is to read those weights as an ``amplitude'' by taking some power of them ---
canonically a square root, so that squaring later recovers a probability. This move is
rarely audited for its own sake: it is usually adopted because it is the operation a
target theory (quantum mechanics, most often) is expected to need, not because the
branching structure itself selects it.

This note isolates exactly when such a power lift is compatible with the branching
structure that produced the weights in the first place, independently of what the lift
is eventually used for. The compatibility condition we use is elementary and
theory-neutral: the lifted weight of a history should be recoverable by summing the
lifted weights of its one-step admissible extensions, exactly as an ordinary
probability measure on a growing history space must satisfy the marginal consistency
required by the Kolmogorov extension theorem~\cite{Billingsley1995}. We call this
property \emph{Kolmogorov consistency} for a family of history weights (Definition
\ref{def:consistency}).

The main result (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) is that Kolmogorov consistency holds for the
power-lift family $\mu^\alpha$ of a finite branching measure $\mu$ if and only if
$\alpha=1$, at every state of the system with at least two admissible successors. In
particular the square-root lift, $\alpha=1/2$, fails this consistency whenever the
system branches at all. The obstruction is elementary, general, and has nothing to do
with phase, complex numbers, or the Born rule: it is a fact about positive numbers and
sums.

The motivating context is the Cosmochrony research programme's search for a bridge
between spectral admissibility and quantum amplitude structure. The companion note
\emph{Finite Support and Preparation Criteria for Independent Effective Subsystems}
and its states-and-effects successor~\cite{Beau2026esub,Beau2026steft} close a finite,
classical, simplicial layer of that programme and explicitly leave open ``the natural
next step'': what could constrain an amplitude modulus without conflating exclusion,
mixture, and coherent cancellation. The trajectory-branching
note~\cite{Beau2026tb} supplies a concrete finite branching system --- a non-backtracking
automaton on Heisenberg-group words --- together with a Pell-recurrence history count,
but selects no probability law on that automaton. Under the explicitly named
maximal-entropy/Parry postulate~\textup{H2}, which does select such a law, this note
determines exactly which power lifts of it remain consistent as history weights, and the
answer is: only the trivial one. Sections~\ref{sec:branching}--\ref{sec:main} state and
prove this in general, self-contained terms; Sections~\ref{sec:h2}--\ref{sec:search}
instantiate it exactly on the Heisenberg automaton and report the accompanying exact
quotient and cancellation analysis; Section~\ref{sec:outlook} states precisely what is
and is not established.

\subsection{Contributions and non-claims}
\label{sec:claims}

This paper establishes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item a general, self-contained theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}): for any finite
  branching system, the power-lift family $\mu^\alpha$ of its induced valuation is
  Kolmogorov-consistent if and only if $\alpha=1$, at every branching state;
  \item an exact instantiation of this obstruction, in $\Qroot$, for the
  maximal-entropy/Parry law of the Heisenberg $b$-shadow automaton under hypothesis
  \textup{H2} (Section~\ref{sec:h2});
  \item exact witnesses, on words of length up to~$4$, that the endpoint quotient and the
  shadow quotient of the automaton are mutually incomparable (Section~\ref{sec:quotients});
  \item two exact covariance checks of the uniform-probe phase diagnostic, and a bounded
  exact search, on words of length up to~$8$, finding no destructive cancellation among
  co-endpoint histories (Section~\ref{sec:search}).
\end{enumerate}

This paper does \emph{not} establish, and does not use as a hypothesis anywhere above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item the Born rule, or any rule mapping an amplitude to an observable probability;
  \item a complex Hilbert-space carrier, or any claim about which scalar field is
  physically selected;
  \item a coherent-sum construction, canonical or otherwise, on any quotient of the
  automaton;
  \item the physical status of hypothesis~\textup{H2} itself, which is stated and used
  exactly as what it is: a named, non-derived postulate (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:h2});
  \item a re-derivation of the $D4$ projective-distinguishability channel
  of~\cite{Beau2026tb}, whose already-proved equivalence to the shadow quotient
  (for a generic probe, pre-saturation) is cited, not repeated.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Finite Branching Systems and Power Lifts}
\label{sec:branching}

\begin{definition}[Finite branching system]
  \label{def:system}
  A \emph{finite branching system} is a triple $(\Sh, M, P)$ where $\Sh$ is a finite set
  of local states, $M \in \{0,1\}^{\Sh \times \Sh}$ is an admissibility matrix, and $P$
  is a row-stochastic matrix on $\Sh$ compatible with $M$: $P_{ij} \ge 0$ for all
  $i,j$, $P_{ij}=0$ whenever $M_{ij}=0$, and $\sum_{j \in \Sh} P_{ij} = 1$ for every
  $i \in \Sh$. State $i$ is a \emph{branching state} if it has at least two states $j$
  with $M_{ij}=1$ and $P_{ij}>0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Admissible histories and induced valuation]
  \label{def:history}
  An \emph{admissible history} of length $n \ge 1$ is a word
  $s=(s_1,\dots,s_n) \in \Sh^n$ with $M_{s_{k-1}s_k}=1$ for $k=2,\dots,n$. Given an
  initial law $\nu$ on $\Sh$, the \emph{induced valuation} of $s$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mu}
    \mu_n(s) \;=\; \nu(s_1) \prod_{k=2}^{n} P_{s_{k-1} s_k}.
  \end{equation}
  For an admissible history $s$ of length $n$ and a state $j$ with $M_{s_n j}=1$, write
  $s \cdot j$ for the length-$(n+1)$ extension of $s$ by $j$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Kolmogorov consistency]
  \label{def:consistency}
  A family $\{w_n\}_{n \ge 1}$ of nonnegative functions on admissible histories, one for
  each length, is \emph{Kolmogorov-consistent} if for every admissible history $s$ of
  length $n$,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:consistency}
    \sum_{j:\, M_{s_n j}=1} w_{n+1}(s \cdot j) \;=\; w_n(s).
  \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why this is the right condition]
  \label{rem:why-kolmogorov}
  Condition~\eqref{eq:consistency} is exactly the finite-dimensional marginal
  consistency required by the Kolmogorov extension theorem for a family of measures on
  cylinder sets of a path space to arise from a single measure on the full (possibly
  infinite) history space~\cite{Billingsley1995}. A family of history weights that
  fails~\eqref{eq:consistency} is not merely inconvenient: it is not the restriction of
  any single measure on histories of unbounded length, and the length-$n$ weights
  $w_n$ cannot be read as the marginals of one another.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The induced valuation is Kolmogorov-consistent]
  \label{prop:mu-consistent}
  The family $\{\mu_n\}_n$ of Definition~\ref{def:history} is Kolmogorov-consistent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  By~\eqref{eq:mu}, $\mu_{n+1}(s \cdot j) = \mu_n(s)\, P_{s_n j}$. Summing over
  admissible $j$ and using row-stochasticity of $P$,
  $\sum_j \mu_{n+1}(s\cdot j) = \mu_n(s) \sum_j P_{s_n j} = \mu_n(s)$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:mu-consistent} is not the content of this paper: it is immediate
from the definition of $\mu_n$, and it is recorded only as the baseline against which
the power-lift family is measured.

\begin{definition}[Power lift]
  \label{def:lift}
  For $\alpha > 0$, the \emph{$\alpha$-power lift} of $\{\mu_n\}_n$ is the family
  $\{\mu_n^\alpha\}_n$, where $\mu_n^\alpha(s) := \mu_n(s)^\alpha$.
\end{definition}

Because $\mu_n(s)$ is a product, $\mu_n(s)^\alpha = \nu(s_1)^\alpha
\prod_{k=2}^n P_{s_{k-1}s_k}^\alpha$: the power lift of the path valuation is the same
object as the valuation induced by the pointwise-lifted kernel $P^\alpha$ (with the
convention $0^\alpha=0$) and initial law $\nu^\alpha$, up to the overall normalisation
addressed in Section~\ref{sec:main}. The square-root case $\alpha=1/2$ is the
canonical candidate for reading $\mu$ as an amplitude modulus, since $\vert \mu^{1/2}
\vert^2 = \mu$.

\section{The Refinement-Consistency Theorem}
\label{sec:main}

\begin{lemma}[Elementary power comparison]
  \label{lem:power-compare}
  Let $x \in [0,1]$ and $\alpha>0$, $\alpha \ne 1$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item If $0<\alpha<1$, then $x^\alpha \ge x$, with equality iff $x \in \{0,1\}$.
    \item If $\alpha>1$, then $x^\alpha \le x$, with equality iff $x \in \{0,1\}$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Both statements are trivial at $x \in \{0,1\}$. For $x \in (0,1)$, write
  $x^\alpha = x \cdot x^{\alpha-1}$. If $0<\alpha<1$ then $\alpha-1<0$, and raising
  $x\in(0,1)$ to a negative power strictly increases it above~$1$, so
  $x^{\alpha-1}>1$ and $x^\alpha>x$. If $\alpha>1$ then $\alpha-1>0$, and raising
  $x \in (0,1)$ to a positive power strictly decreases it below~$1$, so
  $x^{\alpha-1}<1$ and $x^\alpha<x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Refinement consistency forces $\alpha=1$]
  \label{thm:main}
  Let $(\Sh,M,P)$ be a finite branching system and let $i \in \Sh$ be a branching state
  in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:system}. Then for every $\alpha>0$, $\alpha\ne1$,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:main}
    \sum_{j:\,M_{ij}=1} P_{ij}^\alpha \;\ne\; 1 \;=\; \sum_{j:\,M_{ij}=1} P_{ij},
  \end{equation}
  strictly greater than $1$ if $0<\alpha<1$ and strictly less than $1$ if $\alpha>1$.
  Consequently the $\alpha$-power-lift family $\{\mu_n^\alpha\}_n$ of
  Definition~\ref{def:lift} is Kolmogorov-consistent (Definition~\ref{def:consistency})
  if and only if $\alpha=1$, or the system has no branching state reachable within the
  histories considered.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Fix a branching state $i$; write $p_j := P_{ij}$ for the admissible successors
  $j$, so $p_j \in (0,1]$ for at least two indices $j$ (branching) and possibly
  $p_j = 0$ for the rest, with $\sum_j p_j = 1$. Applying
  Lemma~\ref{lem:power-compare} termwise to each $p_j \in [0,1]$ and summing,
  $\sum_j p_j^\alpha \gtrless \sum_j p_j = 1$ with the direction fixed by whether
  $\alpha \lessgtr 1$; the inequality is strict because at least two of the $p_j$ lie
  strictly in $(0,1)$ (branching), and Lemma~\ref{lem:power-compare}'s inequality is
  strict there. This proves~\eqref{eq:main}.

  For the consequence: by the same computation as
  Proposition~\ref{prop:mu-consistent} applied to $\mu^\alpha$ in place of $\mu$,
  \begin{equation*}
    \sum_{j:\,M_{s_n j}=1} \mu_{n+1}^\alpha(s\cdot j)
    = \mu_n(s)^\alpha \sum_{j} P_{s_n j}^\alpha .
  \end{equation*}
  This equals $\mu_n(s)^\alpha = \mu_n^\alpha(s)$ for every admissible history $s$ with
  $\mu_n(s)>0$ if and only if $\sum_j P_{s_n j}^\alpha = 1$ for every branching state
  $s_n$ reachable by such a history; by~\eqref{eq:main} this holds for every $\alpha
  \ne 1$ only in the degenerate case that no branching state is reachable, and holds
  unconditionally for $\alpha=1$ by row-stochasticity of $P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$\mu$ is the unique Kolmogorov-consistent member of its power family]
  \label{cor:unique}
  For any finite branching system with at least one branching state reachable by an
  admissible history of positive $\mu$-valuation, the induced valuation $\mu$ itself is
  the unique member of $\{\mu^\alpha : \alpha>0\}$ that is Kolmogorov-consistent.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[What Theorem~\ref{thm:main} does and does not say]
  \label{rem:scope}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} does not say that a power lift is mathematically
  ill-defined, nor that no theory can ever use one: it says precisely that the lift
  fails to be a projective family of measures in the Kolmogorov sense whenever the
  system branches. A theory that uses $\mu^\alpha$, $\alpha\ne1$, as a physical
  quantity must therefore either abandon Kolmogorov consistency as a requirement on
  that quantity --- and justify doing so independently --- or supply a genuinely
  different construction, not of the pointwise power-lift form of
  Definition~\ref{def:lift}, that restores it. Neither option is exercised in this
  paper; see Section~\ref{sec:outlook}.
\end{remark}

\section{Specialisation: the Heisenberg Shadow Automaton and \textup{H2}}
\label{sec:h2}

\subsection{The $b$-shadow automaton}
\label{sec:automaton}

We recall, without re-proof, the finite branching system supplied
by~\cite{Beau2026tb}. Trajectories are non-backtracking words
$\gamma=(g_1,\dots,g_n)$ over the Heisenberg generating set
$S=\{X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}\}$, identified with paths $u_0=e$, $u_k=u_{k-1}g_k$ in
$\Heis$. The \emph{$b$-shadow} emission alphabet is $\Sh=\{0,+,-\}$: $X^{\pm1}$ emit
$0$, $Y$ emits $+$, $Y^{-1}$ emits $-$. Non-backtracking forbids only the immediate
letter-inverse pair, which after merging $X,X^{-1}$ into the single class $0$ (a
bijection of the word language, since it preserves every $b$-emission~\cite{Beau2026tb})
yields the admissibility matrix
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:M}
  M \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1&1&1\\ 1&1&0\\ 1&0&1 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \text{states ordered } (0,+,-).
\end{equation}
The number of length-$n$ shadows is the Pell count $N_b(n)=2N_b(n-1)+N_b(n-2)$,
$N_b(1)=3$, $N_b(2)=7$~\cite{Beau2026tb}. The matrix $M$ is primitive and symmetric,
with Perron root and right eigenvector
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:perron}
  \lambda = 1+\sqrt2, \qquad r = (\sqrt2,\,1,\,1).
\end{equation}
The trajectory-branching note proves that $M$, the Pell recurrence, and $\lambda,r$ are
exact consequences of the non-backtracking word combinatorics; it proves \emph{no}
normalised transition law, stochastic kernel, or stationary measure on $\Sh$. No such
law is supplied elsewhere in the deposited corpus either: an exact two-parameter
counterfamily of primitive, sign-symmetric Markov kernels on the same support gives
distinct stationary laws, so support, primitivity, and the Pell recurrence alone do not
select one.

\subsection{Hypothesis \textup{H2}}
\label{sec:h2-hyp}

\begin{hypothesis}[\textup{H2}: Parry selection, not derived]
  \label{hyp:h2}
  The physically realised asymptotic measure on the primitive automaton~\eqref{eq:M} is
  the unique shift-invariant measure of maximal entropy, equivalently the Parry measure
  determined by the Perron data~\eqref{eq:perron}~\cite{Parry1964}.
\end{hypothesis}

Hypothesis~\textup{H2} is used in this paper exactly as stated: a named postulate, not a
consequence of the automaton's combinatorics, and not derived from any admissibility or
relaxation result of the wider programme. Under~\textup{H2} alone, the Parry transition
kernel and stationary law are
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:parry-kernel}
  P_{ij} = \frac{M_{ij}\,r_j}{\lambda\, r_i}, \qquad
  \pi_i \propto r_i^2 ,
\end{equation}
which evaluate exactly, in the state order $(0,+,-)$, to
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:P-exact}
  P = \begin{pmatrix}
    \sqrt2-1 & 1-\tfrac12\sqrt2 & 1-\tfrac12\sqrt2 \\
    2-\sqrt2 & \sqrt2-1 & 0 \\
    2-\sqrt2 & 0 & \sqrt2-1
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \pi = \Bigl(\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac14\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Every entry of~\eqref{eq:P-exact} lies in $\Qroot$, and direct summation confirms $P$ is
exactly row-stochastic: for instance
$(\sqrt2-1)+(1-\tfrac12\sqrt2)+(1-\tfrac12\sqrt2) = 1$ identically in $\Qroot$.

The induced valuation of Definition~\ref{def:history}, under the stationary initial law
$\nu=\pi$, is $\mu_n(s) = \pi_{s_1}\prod_{k=2}^n P_{s_{k-1}s_k}$, an element of $\Qroot$
for every admissible shadow $s$.

\subsection{Exact instantiation in $\Qroot$}
\label{sec:exact-instantiation}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} already settles, in general terms, that no $\alpha\ne1$ power
lift of this $\mu$ is Kolmogorov-consistent. We record, independently, an exact
computation for $\alpha=1/2$ in $\Qroot$, both as a concrete instance and as a
cross-check of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} by a different method: sign analysis of an exact
algebraic identity, rather than the elementary order comparison of
Lemma~\ref{lem:power-compare}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact square-root failure on the shadow automaton]
  \label{prop:sqrt-fail}
  For every row $i\in\{0,+,-\}$ of~\eqref{eq:P-exact}, $\sum_j \sqrt{P_{ij}} > 1$
  strictly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Write $x_j=\sqrt{P_{ij}}\ge0$ for the nonzero entries of row $i$. Then
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cross}
    \Bigl(\sum_j x_j\Bigr)^{\!2}
    = \sum_j x_j^2 + 2\!\!\sum_{j<k} x_j x_k
    = \sum_j P_{ij} + 2\!\!\sum_{j<k} \sqrt{P_{ij}P_{ik}}
    = 1 + 2\!\!\sum_{j<k} \sqrt{P_{ij}P_{ik}}.
  \end{equation}
  Every row of~\eqref{eq:P-exact} has at least two nonzero entries, so at least one
  cross term $\sqrt{P_{ij}P_{ik}}$ appears in~\eqref{eq:cross}; each product
  $P_{ij}P_{ik}$ is a product of two positive elements of $\Qroot$
  ($\sqrt2-1,\ 1-\tfrac12\sqrt2,\ 2-\sqrt2$ are each positive, exactly checked by the
  sign test $p^2 \gtrless 2q^2$ on $p+q\sqrt2$), hence itself positive, and the cross
  term is a strictly positive real number. Thus $(\sum_j x_j)^2 > 1$, and since
  $\sum_j x_j \ge 0$, $\sum_j x_j > 1$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:sqrt-fail} recovers the $\alpha=1/2$ case of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for this specific $P$ by an independent argument, and computes
it in exact $\Qroot$ arithmetic rather than by the general order comparison.

\section{Endpoint and Shadow Quotients Are Incomparable}
\label{sec:quotients}

This section records a second, independent structural fact about the automaton, used
only for the covariance and search analysis of Section~\ref{sec:search} and not for the
refinement-consistency result of Sections~\ref{sec:branching}--\ref{sec:h2}.

We use the Heisenberg group law and Weil action of the deposited spectral-admissibility
pipeline~\cite{Beau2026tb,Beau2026a37}:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:heis-law}
  (a,b,\gamma)(a',b',\gamma') = (a+a',\,b+b',\,\gamma+\gamma'+ab') \bmod q,
  \qquad
  \bigl[\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)f\bigr](x) = \psi_c(\gamma+bx)\,f(x+a),
\end{equation}
with generators $X=(1,0,0)$, $Y=(0,1,0)$ and inverses
$(a,b,\gamma)^{-1}=(-a,-b,ab-\gamma)$.

\begin{definition}[Endpoint and shadow quotients]
  \label{def:quotients}
  For an admissible word $\gamma=(g_1,\dots,g_n)$ with path endpoint
  $u_n=(a_n,b_n,\gamma_n) \in \Heis$ and $b$-shadow $s=(s_1,\dots,s_n)\in\Sh^n$, the
  \emph{endpoint quotient} is $\pi_{D1}(\gamma):=u_n$ and the \emph{shadow quotient} is
  $\pi_{\mathrm{shadow}}(\gamma):=s$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Incomparability]
  \label{prop:incomparable}
  Neither $\pi_{D1}$ nor $\pi_{\mathrm{shadow}}$ refines the other: there exist words of
  equal length with the same shadow and distinct endpoints, and words of equal length
  with the same endpoint and distinct shadows.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof by exact witness]
  For the first claim, at $n=1$ the words $X$ and $X^{-1}$ share the shadow $(0)$ and
  have endpoints $(1,0,0)$ and $(q{-}1,0,0)$ respectively, distinct for every $q>2$.

  For the second claim, take $q=17$. The words
  $\gamma_1=(X,X,Y,X^{-1})$ and $\gamma_2=(Y^{-1},X,Y,Y)$ are both admissible
  (non-backtracking) of length~$4$, with shadows
  $s_1=(0,0,+,0)$ and $s_2=(-,0,+,+)$, $s_1 \ne s_2$. Direct application
  of~\eqref{eq:heis-law} gives, step by step,
  \begin{align*}
    \gamma_1&:\ (0,0,0) \to (1,0,0) \to (2,0,0) \to (2,1,2) \to (1,1,2), \\
    \gamma_2&:\ (0,0,0) \to (0,16,0) \to (1,16,0) \to (1,0,1) \to (1,1,2),
  \end{align*}
  reaching the same endpoint $(1,1,2)$ from distinct shadows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Both directions are structurally forced, not accidental]
  \label{rem:incomparable-why}
  A fixed shadow class merges every choice of sign for its $0$-emission steps ($X$ vs
  $X^{-1}$), which changes $a_n$ and, through the cocycle in~\eqref{eq:heis-law},
  generically $\gamma_n$: the shadow quotient is a genuine coarsening of the endpoint
  along that axis. Conversely the endpoint retains only $(a_n,b_n,\gamma_n)$, a
  three-dimensional summary of an $n$-step word, so distinct step orders reaching the
  same net displacement and swept area are expected generically for $n$ large enough;
  Proposition~\ref{prop:incomparable} exhibits this already at $n=4$.
\end{remark}

\section{Covariance and a Bounded Exact Cancellation Search}
\label{sec:search}

\subsection{The uniform-probe phase diagnostic}

On the uniform probe, the action in~\eqref{eq:heis-law} gives
$\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)|\mathrm{unif}\rangle = \psi_c(\gamma)\,e_{cb}$, a global phase
$\psi_c(\gamma)$ times the character $e_{cb}$. For two admissible words sharing the
same final $b_n$ (hence the same character direction), the relative phase of their
uniform-probe amplitudes is $\psi_c(\gamma_n-\gamma_n')$, an exact $q$-th root of
unity.

\begin{proposition}[Conjugate covariance]
  \label{prop:conjugate}
  $\psi_{q-c}(\gamma) = \overline{\psi_c(\gamma)}$ for every $\gamma$. Consequently any
  exact vanishing of a weighted sum $\sum_h \mu_n(h)\,\psi_c(\gamma_n(h))$ over a fixed
  co-$b_n$ class holds for character $c$ if and only if it holds for the conjugate
  character $q-c$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  $\psi_{q-c}(\gamma)=e^{2\pi i (q-c)\gamma/q}=e^{2\pi i\gamma}e^{-2\pi ic\gamma/q}
  =\overline{\psi_c(\gamma)}$ since $\gamma\in\Z$. The stated sum for character $q-c$ is
  therefore the complex conjugate of the sum for $c$, and a complex number vanishes iff
  its conjugate does.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generator-relabelling covariance]
  \label{prop:relabel}
  Relabelling $X\leftrightarrow X^{-1}$ throughout an admissible word leaves its
  $b$-shadow, and hence its induced valuation $\mu_n$, exactly unchanged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Both $X$ and $X^{-1}$ emit the same shadow symbol $0$, and the relabelling is a
  bijection of the non-backtracking word language~\cite{Beau2026tb}: it maps admissible
  words to admissible words letter by letter, without altering any emission.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact cancellation criterion}

\begin{lemma}[Cyclotomic vanishing over $\Qroot$, $q=17$]
  \label{lem:cyclotomic}
  Let $q=17$ and let $\zeta$ be a primitive $q$-th root of unity. For coefficients
  $c_0,\dots,c_{q-1} \in \Qroot$,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cyclotomic}
    \sum_{k=0}^{q-1} c_k \zeta^k = 0
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    c_0=c_1=\cdots=c_{q-1}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Since $q=17$ is prime, the minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\Q$ is the cyclotomic
  polynomial $\Phi_{17}(x)=1+x+\cdots+x^{16}$, of degree $q-1=16=[\Q(\zeta):\Q]$; the
  unique $\Q$-linear relation among $1,\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{16}$ is (a scalar multiple of)
  $\Phi_{17}(\zeta)=0$. The Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\Q(\zeta)/\Q)$ is cyclic of order
  $16$, so $\Q(\zeta)$ has a unique quadratic subfield, namely $\Q(\sqrt{q^*})$ with
  $q^*=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}q$; for $q=17$, $(q-1)/2=8$ is even, so $q^*=17$ and the subfield
  is $\Q(\sqrt{17})$, not $\Qroot$. Since $[\Qroot:\Q]=2$ is prime and $\Qroot \not\subset
  \Q(\zeta)$, the intersection $\Qroot \cap \Q(\zeta)$ is exactly $\Q$; as $\Q(\zeta)/\Q$
  is Galois, this makes $\Qroot$ and $\Q(\zeta)$ linearly disjoint over $\Q$, so
  $[\Qroot(\zeta):\Qroot]=[\Q(\zeta):\Q]=16$ and $\zeta$ retains the same minimal
  polynomial $\Phi_{17}$ over $\Qroot$. Hence the only $\Qroot$-linear relation among
  $1,\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{16}$ is again the all-ones relation, up to a scalar in $\Qroot$,
  proving~\eqref{eq:cyclotomic}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Bounded exact search}

Using Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclotomic}, exact cancellation of the weighted uniform-probe sum
$\sum_h \mu_n(h)\,\psi_c(\gamma_n(h))$ over a class of admissible histories sharing a
final $b_n$ is decided by grouping the class's $\mu_n$-weights by residue
$k=(c\gamma_n) \bmod q$ and checking whether the resulting $q$ coefficients (padding
absent residues with $0$) are all equal.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded negative search result]
  \label{prop:search}
  For $q=17$, $c=1$, and all admissible words of length $n \le 8$ ($13120$ words
  total), there are $64$ classes of $\ge2$ words sharing a final $b_n$; $62$ of these
  carry more than one distinct value of $\gamma_n \bmod q$ among their members (a
  genuine, non-vacuous relative phase); and among all $64$ classes, exact vanishing in
  the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclotomic} occurs in none.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Computational certification]
  Exhaustive exact enumeration; see Section~\ref{sec:certification}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reading Proposition~\ref{prop:search}]
  \label{rem:search-reading}
  This is a calibrated negative result on a bounded range, not a claim of non-existence
  at every $n$: it states that exact destructive cancellation among co-endpoint
  admissible histories, weighted by the~\textup{H2} valuation, does not occur for
  $n\le8$ at $q=17$, despite genuine relative phase being present in $62$ of the $64$
  qualifying classes.
\end{remark}

\section{Computational Certification}
\label{sec:certification}

One script is archived with this note, in \texttt{code/}, using exact arithmetic
throughout (Python's \texttt{fractions.Fraction} and a stdlib-only $\Qroot$ class; no
floating-point computation enters any claim of this paper) and no third-party
dependency.

\texttt{history\_measure\_enumeration.py} certifies: the exact row-stochasticity of $P$
in~\eqref{eq:P-exact}; the exact positive-cross-term proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sqrt-fail} for all three rows of $P$; enumeration of all $13120$
non-backtracking words of length $n \le 8$ over $\Heis(\Z/17\Z)$, their exact endpoints
and shadows; both witnesses of Proposition~\ref{prop:incomparable} (found by exhaustive
search over the enumerated set, then re-verified by hand against~\eqref{eq:heis-law}
step by step); the exact cyclotomic-vanishing search of
Proposition~\ref{prop:search} for $c=1$ and, independently, for the conjugate
$c=16=q-c$, confirming Proposition~\ref{prop:conjugate}'s predicted covariance of the
vanishing pattern; and the shadow-invariance of Proposition~\ref{prop:relabel} for all
$13120$ enumerated words. All assertions pass; no numerical claim in this paper exceeds
what the script checks.

\section{Limits, Non-Claims, and Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

The following are explicitly \emph{not} established by this paper, restated here for
clarity alongside Section~\ref{sec:claims}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No Born rule.} No map from a valuation, a lift, or a coherent sum to an
  observable probability is proposed or used anywhere in this paper.
  \item \textbf{No complex-carrier claim.} The uniform-probe phase diagnostic of
  Section~\ref{sec:search} uses the deposited Weil action of~\cite{Beau2026tb} as
  supplied; this paper derives none of its complex structure and makes no claim that
  this structure is physically selected.
  \item \textbf{No coherent-sum construction.} Proposition~\ref{prop:search} is a
  negative existence search on a bounded range, not the construction or endorsement of
  any canonical coherent sum on any quotient of Section~\ref{sec:quotients}.
  \item \textbf{\textup{H2} is a postulate, not a theorem.} Every result of
  Sections~\ref{sec:h2}--\ref{sec:search} is conditional on Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:h2} and
  must be read with that conditionality attached; Section~\ref{sec:main}'s Theorem
  \ref{thm:main} alone is unconditional, general, and independent of \textup{H2}.
  \item \textbf{No re-derivation of $D4$.} The equivalence, for a generic probe on the
  pre-saturation window, of the shadow quotient and the canonical projective
  distinguishability channel of~\cite{Beau2026tb} is cited from that paper's own
  theorem and not reproduced here.
\end{itemize}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:unique} constrain, rather than foreclose,
any future construction of an amplitude modulus for a finite branching system. By
Remark~\ref{rem:scope}, such a construction must either be something other than a
pointwise power lift of the branching valuation, or must explicitly abandon Kolmogorov
consistency as a requirement and justify that choice on independent grounds; a
construction that silently assumes both the branching valuation \emph{and} its
Kolmogorov consistency \emph{and} a nonlinear lift of it is assuming something that
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} shows cannot hold together. This is the precise sense in which
the present obstruction is a necessary condition on, not a substitute for, any future
resolution of the amplitude-modulus question this paper's motivating context leaves
open~\cite{Beau2026steft}.
